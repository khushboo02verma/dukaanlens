Shelf audit in kirana stores: what to measure, and what to ask before you buy software

DukaanLens team · 6 min read

A shelf audit answers one question: when a shopper stands in front of the shelf, can they find your product? In a supermarket that is checked by merchandisers. In a kirana store it usually depends on what a salesman remembers to write down after the visit.

Four things worth measuring

  • On shelf or not: is each product you sell to this store actually there today?
  • Share of shelf: how much of the shelf your brand holds, next to rivals.
  • Planogram: are products where the agreed layout says they should be?
  • Price label: does the label match the price you set?

Why paper and app forms fall short

Forms are filled after the visit, from memory, and they are easy to fill without looking. They also arrive too late: by the time a missing product shows up in a report, the shopper has already bought another brand.

What a photo-based audit changes

With a photo taken in the store, the record is the shelf itself, not someone's memory of it. Software can then count products, compare against the planogram, and flag stores where something is wrong, the same day.

Questions to ask any shelf audit software

  1. Does it work on crowded kirana shelves and phone photos, or only in supermarkets?
  2. How does it stop staged or reused photos (in-app camera only, time and location, surprise re-checks)?
  3. Can it learn your own product range, including new packs?
  4. How soon does the salesman see the result: before leaving the store, or the next day?
  5. Who owns the photos and where are they stored?

This guide is part of a test site. If you are choosing shelf audit software, compare real vendors with the questions above.
